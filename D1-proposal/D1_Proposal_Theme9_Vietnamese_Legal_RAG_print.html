1. Problem

Vietnamese statutory reasoning is not primarily a document retrieval problem. Legal applicability depends on the interaction between regulatory hierarchy, cross-references, amendments, effective dates, and case-specific facts. A single inquiry may therefore require evidence from multiple Laws, Decrees, Circulars, and their amendment chains.

Existing Vietnamese legal RAG systems demonstrate the value of combining semantic retrieval with legal graphs, but their online execution remains largely predefined. For example, SBV-LawGraph uses top-$k$ retrieval followed by a fixed one-hop graph expansion over amendment, repeal, replacement, and guidance relations. Its results show that graph retrieval improves over Naive RAG, but the traversal policy itself remains fixed [1]. This creates three structural limitations:

  1. Context dilution: Fixed retrieval and graph expansion can return related provisions that are not necessary for the current claim, increasing context and citation-selection errors. In SBV-LawGraph, Precision@2 drops to 0.37–0.39 because an omnibus circular often amends dozens of unrelated articles across earlier decrees, injecting >60% distracting noise into the prompt [1].
  2. Using the wrong version of a law: Finding a relevant legal rule is not enough. At the time of the transaction, the rule may have changed, no longer applied, or not yet come into force. Recent research on legal AI agents highlights the need to check which version of a rule applied on the date in question [2].
  3. Limited adaptive reasoning: VLegal-Bench contains 10,450 expert-grounded samples covering retrieval, multi-step reasoning, and scenario-based Vietnamese legal tasks, reflecting the need to reason across multiple pieces of evidence rather than answer from a single retrieved passage [3].

The core problem is therefore adaptive evidence acquisition under changing legal state and incomplete case information.

3. Why an agent: Dynamic control rather than fixed workflow

The distinction is not that a workflow cannot contain loops or branches. A workflow can implement predefined retrieval, retry, and approval paths. The limitation arises when the next retrieval action cannot be specified before the current evidence is observed.

Our system treats the agent as a state-dependent controller with three capabilities:

  1. Dynamic Planning: Decompose the inquiry and determine which legal source, graph relation, or retrieval operation should be executed next. The number and order of retrieval steps are determined during execution.
  2. Closed-loop Critique and Backtracking: A Drafter produces a provisional answer, while an Auditor decomposes it into atomic claims and verifies each claim against retrieved authority. Unsupported or temporally invalid claims trigger targeted re-retrieval and revision. This follows the adaptive retrieval and reflection principle of Self-RAG and the claim-level auditing approach of GANDR [4, 5].
  3. Interactive Disambiguation: When legal applicability depends on missing variables (such as enterprise capital tier or foreign equity ratio), the agent pauses and requests the minimum information required to continue instead of applying an implicit assumption.

The project therefore evaluates whether state-dependent planning and verification improve legal answer quality over fixed RAG and workflow baselines.

4. Candidate technical axes

1. Multi-Agent System Coordination. Role-segregated execution with a Legal Search Agent for statutory retrieval, a Legal Law Update Agent for official validity verification, a Legal Reasoning Agent for statutory synthesis, and a Legal Verification Agent for citation verification. The orchestration layer maintains shared task state and coordinates agent handoffs.

2. Planning & Selective Statutory Traversal. Autonomous query decomposition followed by selective traversal of directional legal relationships such as Amends, Repeals, Replaces, and Guides. Unlike fixed one-hop expansion, traversal depth and path selection depend on the evidence required by the current query [1].

3. Reflection & Statutory Claim Verification. The Reasoning-Verification loop evaluates generated answers against authoritative legal provisions. Failed verification triggers targeted retrieval and revision rather than unconditional regeneration. The design is informed by Self-RAG, SAFE, and GANDR [4, 5, 6].

4. Security Guardrails & Gated Actions. Tool access is controlled through permission tiers. External-source content is treated as untrusted input, while irreversible actions such as dossier export require explicit human authorization. Indirect prompt injection is evaluated separately from the legal reasoning task [7].

5. Proposed Multi-Agent Architecture & Workflow

5.1 System Architecture Overview

The system coordinates five specialized agent roles managed under a supervisor state machine:

  1. Orchestrator Agent (Supervisor): Formulates step-by-step legal research plans, breaks down complex inquiries, routes tasks between search and update agents, and interacts with the user for clarification.
  2. Legal Search Agent: Retrieves relevant statutory provisions and traverses legal cross-references (amendments, guiding circulars, and replacement decrees) to find applicable rules.
  3. Legal Law Update Agent: Queries official legal databases (such as vbpl.vn) to verify the current validity of each statute as of the transaction date, capturing recent amendments [2].
  4. Legal Reasoning Agent: Synthesizes retrieved evidence, regulatory hierarchies, and case-specific facts into actionable legal advice and advisory memos.
  5. Legal Verification Agent: Independently cross-checks every legal claim and statutory citation against the source texts to ensure complete accuracy and eliminate errors before delivery [5, 6].

5.2 End-to-End Multi-Agent Workflow Sequence

Figure 1 illustrates the end-to-end execution flow of the system. Execution proceeds through six coordinated operational phases:

  1. Phase 1 (Inquiry & Context Ingestion): Ingests user legal inquiry, case facts, and client profile parameters (corporate structure, capital tier, foreign ownership).
  2. Phase 2 (Planning & Query Decomposition): Decomposes the inquiry into targeted legal questions and coordinates parallel search and update tasks.
  3. Phase 3 (Statutory Search & Reference Traversal): The Legal Search Agent identifies relevant laws, decrees, and circulars, following reference chains across regulatory regimes.
  4. Phase 4 (Real-Time Validity Check): The Legal Law Update Agent confirms whether cited regulations remain in effect or have been modified by newer rules.
  5. Phase 5 (Legal Reasoning & Verification Loop): The Legal Reasoning Agent drafts the legal opinion. The Legal Verification Agent validates all citations; any ungrounded claim triggers a revision loop (up to 3 iterations).
  6. Phase 6 (User Review & Report Delivery): The verified advisory report is generated, with any official actions requiring explicit user confirmation.

5.4 Memory Hierarchy Design

  • Short-Term Working Memory: In-memory agent state recording multi-turn consultation history, retrieved statutory passages, verification logs, candidate draft states, and retry counters (capped at 3 refinement loops).
  • Long-Term Memory: Persistent SQLite database (legal_advisory.db) maintaining: (i) client and case profiles (corporate structure, capital tier, sector, headcount); (ii) audit logs (timestamped verification results, user confirmations); and (iii) validity cache (cached legal status lookups to ensure fast response times).

6. Evaluation Plan & Baselines

6.1 Benchmark Corpora & Task Suite

  • Statutory Corpus: 1,703 documents (12 Laws, 84 Decrees, 777 Decisions, 763 Circulars) from the SBV Corpus [1], indexed into Neo4j (5,221 nodes, 6,019 edges) and Qdrant dense vector store, augmented by corporate, labor, and tax regulations.
  • Task Set (100 Questions from SBV Legal Dataset [1] + 20 Advanced Tasks): (1) 89 single-document factual lookups; (2) 11 multi-document chained amendment queries; (3) 10 hierarchical multi-tier compliance scenarios (Law → Decree → Circular); (4) 5 contract review and administrative drafting tasks; and (5) 5 adversarial injection and parameter-missing edge cases.

6.2 Evaluation Metrics & Formulations

  1. Retrieval Recall, Precision@2, and F2-Score:
    Precision@k = |R̂ik ∩ Ri| / k,     Recall@k = |R̂ik ∩ Ri| / |Ri|,     F2@k = (5 · Precision@k · Recall@k) / (4 · Precision@k + Recall@k)
    measuring whether selective edge traversal eliminates false-positive noise over SBV-LawGraph's 0.37–0.39 baseline [1].
  2. Strict Answer Correctness:
    Correctness = (1 / N) ∑ Correct(ai, gi)
    requiring semantic equivalence, exact article citation, and verified official legal validity [9].
  3. Per-Claim Grounding Rate (SAFE [6] / Ragas Faithfulness [10]):
    Grounding Rate = (Supported Atomic Propositions) / (Total Generated Legal Claims)
  4. Operational Envelope: End-to-end response latency ≤ 12 s and API cost ≤ $0.02 per complex query.

6.3 Baselines & Ablation Configurations

  • Baselines: (1) Naive Chunk RAG; (2) Static Graph-RAG (SBV-LawGraph [1], ViHERMES [11]); (3) Single-Agent ReAct.
  • Ablations: (1) Full Multi-Agent System; (2) Without Legal Verification Agent; (3) Without Selective Reference Traversal; and (4) Without Legal Law Update Agent (offline store only).
  • Protocol: Evaluated across ≥ 3 random seeds, reporting mean ± standard deviation.

8. Risks, Budget & Google ADK Resource Estimation

8.1 Risks & Mitigation Fallbacks

  • External Portal Rate Limits & Latency: Caches frequent statutory status lookups locally in SQLite with a 24-hour TTL; falls back to offline indexed store if portal requests exceed 3 seconds.
  • Statutory Clause Ambiguity: If a circular omits quantitative operational thresholds, the Legal Verification Agent flags the ambiguity and triggers user interaction rather than extrapolating.
