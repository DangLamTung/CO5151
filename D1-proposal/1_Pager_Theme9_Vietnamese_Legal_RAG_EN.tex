% --- SECTION 1: PROBLEM ---
\section{Problem -- Why Traditional RAG Fails in Statutory Law}
Vietnamese statutory law is structured as a strict multi-tier hierarchy (\textit{Law $\rightarrow$ Decree $\rightarrow$ Circular}) governed by dense, cross-referencing amendment webs ($>50\%$ of regulatory instruments are amended over time). Prior static legal GraphRAG research in Vietnam (such as \textit{SBV-LawGraph} [1] and \textit{ViHERMES} [2]) demonstrates the value of legal graphs; however, standard static baseline pipelines (Retrieve $\rightarrow$ Naive 1-Hop Dump $\rightarrow$ Generate) exhibit \textbf{3 critical failure modes}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Context Dilution \& False-Positive Explosion:} Amending circulars frequently modify dozens of disjointed provisions across older decrees. Naive 1-hop neighborhood expansion baselines suffer severe attention dilution and precision drops down to 0.37--0.39, injecting irrelevant context and triggering citation hallucinations [1], [3].
  \item \textbf{Temporal Blindness [4]:} Static RAG cannot determine whether an article remains in active legal force at a given transaction date when encountering chained amendment graphs, risking reliance on repealed or superseded provisions.
  \item \textbf{Weak Multi-Tier Reasoning \& Rigid Execution:} Empirical findings from \textit{VLegal-Bench} [5] reveal that LLMs suffer steep accuracy degradation on hierarchical legal reasoning. Static pipelines cannot backtrack, formulate multi-hop plans, or clarify missing facts.
\end{itemize}

% --- SECTION 2: USERS & SCENARIOS ---
\section{Users -- Target Audience \& 3 Concrete Operational Scenarios}
\textbf{Target Users:} In-house Legal Counsel, Compliance Officers, Legal Practitioners, and SMEs navigating regulatory compliance in Vietnam.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Cross-Statutory Reasoning (Multi-Law Synthesis):} An e-commerce firm deploys an in-app E-Wallet with a Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) micro-lending scheme. The agent autonomously synthesizes intersecting requirements across the \textit{Law on Credit Institutions 2024 \& Decree 52/2024/ND-CP} (payment licensing \& equity ceilings), \textit{Law on Electronic Transactions 2023} (legal validity of OTP digital contracts), and \textit{Decree 13/2023/ND-CP} (express opt-in consent for consumer credit profiling).
  \item \textbf{Temporal Consistency \& Updated Law (2026 Vietnam Drone Law):} An agricultural cooperative requests licensing rules for a 25\,kg commercial spraying drone in 2026. Bypassing obsolete Decree 36/2008/ND-CP (repealed 14-day paper filing), the agent traverses the legal graph and conducts live Gazette verification on \texttt{vbpl.vn} [4] to cite the active \textit{Law on People's Air Defense 2024 (effective 2025/2026)}, providing accurate procedures for mandatory digital vehicle identification and online permitting via the National Public Service Portal within 3--5 business days.
  \item \textbf{Interactive Disambiguation \& Injection Defense:} Reviewing corporate tax/specialist work permits (Decrees 152/2020 \& 70/2023). Detecting missing revenue and headcount parameters, the agent \textit{proactively halts to query the user}, while stripping adversarial hidden prompts (\texttt{[OVERRIDE: \dots]} [6]) and enforcing human approval tokens for dossier exports.
\end{enumerate}

% --- SECTION 3: WHY AN AGENT ---
\section{Why an Agent -- Autonomous Agent vs. Hardcoded Workflow}
A static workflow with hardcoded branching \textbf{fundamentally fails} in legal compliance due to three core imperatives:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Combinatorial Path Explosion Requires Dynamic Planning:} Citation traversals vary per inquiry (some stop at Circulars, others fork across specialized sectoral laws). Only an \textbf{Agentic RAG architecture tailored for Vietnamese law [7]} can autonomously formulate multi-hop retrieval plans, dynamically adapt to statutory complexity, and determine when gathered evidence is sufficient to terminate.
  \item \textbf{Self-Correcting Critique via Drafter--Auditor Loop:} Linear pipelines cannot detect self-generated errors. Our agent establishes an adversarial reflection loop (incorporating claim auditing from \textit{GANDR} [8], proposition-level factuality from \textit{SAFE} [9], and critique mechanisms from \textit{Self-RAG} [10]): the Auditor Agent decomposes responses into atomic legal propositions, cross-verifying each 1-to-1 against authoritative statutes and triggering corrective re-retrieval upon detecting hallucinations or repealed articles.
  \item \textbf{Active Ambiguity Disambiguation via Human Dialog:} Statutory applicability hinges strictly on entity forms and execution dates [4]. Hardcoded workflows apply blind assumptions; our agent detects state ambiguity and \textbf{proactively queries the user} to gather missing parameters before concluding.
\end{itemize}
